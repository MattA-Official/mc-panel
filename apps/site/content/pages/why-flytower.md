# Why “Flytower”?

Flytower started at a student theatre. It was built to run the Minecraft server for the [Nottingham New Theatre](https://www.newtheatre.org.uk), so when it needed a name, it took one from the building rather than from the game.

## What a fly tower is

Look at a theatre from the outside and you will often see a tall, plain block rising above the stage. That is the fly tower. Inside it, scenery hangs on long metal bars called battens, high above the audience's sightline. When a scene changes, a crew member pulls a line and a whole set flies in on cue, ready to play. When it is done, it flies back out of sight. A busy show can have dozens of pieces waiting up there at once.

## Why it fits

That is what Flytower does for Minecraft servers. Several servers hang in one box, each on its own batten: its own user, folder, memory and address. You bring one in, take one out, or swap in a new world, all from the panel, without anyone climbing up into the rigging with SSH.

The logo is that picture: three battens under the grid, with the lowest one lit in amber because it is the one “in” right now.

## Why not something blocky

Minecraft already has a look, and it belongs to Mojang. Rather than imitate it with grass, dirt and pixels, Flytower borrows the backstage palette: amber like a tungsten work lamp, cool steel like the rigging, a green “go” for a running server and a blue backstage light for notices. It also means Flytower never has to pretend to be an official Minecraft product, because it isn't one.
